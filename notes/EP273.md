# Problem 273: what the structure looks like

Is there a covering system with distinct moduli m = p - 1, p >= 5 prime? Open; nothing here settles it.

## Reduction to three coupled coverings

Every modulus is even, and m = 4 covers one class mod 4, say x = 0. On a class x = 4w + c:

- m = 4r (so 4r + 1 is prime) lies inside one class mod 4 and acts on w as the modulus r;
- m = 2q with q odd (so 2q + 1 is prime) lies inside one parity and acts as the modulus q on both
  classes of that parity. The two residues differ by -1/2 mod q, which is one fixed translation of w
  for all such q together.

So a covering exists exactly when there are three coverings of the integers w:

1. class 2: moduli r from R = {r >= 3 : 4r + 1 prime} = {3, 4, 7, 9, 10, 13, 15, 18, 22, 24, ...},
   plus any odd q given to the even side;
2. classes 1 and 3: one shared set of odd q from Q = {q odd : 2q + 1 prime} = {3, 5, 9, 11, 15, 21, ...},
   and for each class its own set of r, the three sets of r pairwise disjoint.

A value can occur twice in one class (3 is in both R and Q), which is what makes closing a class easy.

## What exists at small lcm (`search/ep273/class_cover.py`, exact depth-first search)

- One odd class on its own is easy: nine moduli with lcm 144,
  r in {3, 4, 9, 18, 24, 48, 144} and q in {3, 9}. The search finds it in 9 nodes.
- A covering from R alone (the cheapest way to do class 2) was not found for lcm up to 90,720 within
  300,000 search nodes per lcm. That is "gave up", not "does not exist". A longer run with 15,000,000
  nodes per lcm also gave up at lcm 15,120 (33 moduli, sum 1.326) and 30,240 (39 moduli, sum 1.339);
  see `results/ep273-r-only.txt`.

## Why the full problem is still hard

The three classes compete for R. The easy single-class covering spends 3, 4, 9, 18, 24, 48, 144, and
there is no second copy of those for the other two classes. The exhaustive result on the forum (no
covering with lcm below 8e6) says the three cannot be completed together at small lcm.

Budget in these terms: with S_R and S_Q the reciprocal sums of the two pools, a covering needs
S_R + 2 S_Q >= 3 plus overlap. At lcm 8.5e21 the pool gives about 4.68, so about 0.56 of overlap per
class is affordable. This is more room than the plain count (1.42 against 1) suggests, because every
odd q is used twice. It is the one encouraging number here.

## Not done

No exact search over the coupled problem, and no structured (tree) construction at large lcm, which
is what the numbers above say is needed. The even half in `half_cover.py` (cost 0.852) is
superseded by this split as the thing to optimise.

## On paper: what any covering must satisfy (2026-11-18)

Notation: R1, R2, R3 are the sets of r used on classes 1, 2, 3; Qo, Qe the odd q used on the odd side
and on the even side. rho_c = sum of 1/r over R_c, sigma_o and sigma_e the sums of 1/q.

**The reduction is an equivalence.** Given a covering, sort each modulus by its residue: m = 4r lies in
one class mod 4, m = 2q in one parity. Conversely three coverings of w as described lift back, because
for odd q the class x = a (mod 2q) meets x = 4w + c in w = (a - c)/4 (mod q). For the shared q the
residues in classes 1 and 3 differ by -1/2 mod q; one integer shift of w realises that for every q at
once (the lcm of the q is odd), and shifting w preserves "is a covering". So the shared q can be given
the same residue in both odd classes.

**Cost of the even side.** Each class needs reciprocal mass at least 1:
rho_1 + sigma_o >= 1, rho_3 + sigma_o >= 1, rho_2 + sigma_e >= 1. The total over the original moduli is
1/4 + (rho_1 + rho_2 + rho_3)/4 + (sigma_o + sigma_e)/2, so

    sum of 1/m  >=  1 + sigma_e / 4.

Every odd q spent on the even side is half wasted. The cheapest even half therefore uses no odd q:
m = 4 plus a covering of class 2 by moduli 4r alone, cost 1/4 + rho_2/4, which is above 1/2 and equals
1/2 only for an exact tiling. So the first aimed question is whether R alone covers Z. Greedy gets R to
2.5% uncovered at lcm 2e7 (table above), so this piece looks reachable at large lcm, but then it uses
nearly all of R.

**The coupling is the whole difficulty.** Drop the requirement that R1, R2, R3 be disjoint and each
class is covered by nine moduli. So any proof of impossibility, and any search, has to be about how the
small r (3, 4, 9, 18, 24, 27, 48) are divided among three classes that each want them.

**The same split repeats at every prime.** Fix a prime l and one of the three coverings. Its moduli not
divisible by l give the same pattern, up to a shift, on each of the l residue classes mod l; a modulus
divisible by l lies in one residue class. So inside every class the problem is again "one shared
pattern, l private families that must each finish it". For l = 3 the private moduli are those divisible
by 3, and r = 3 or q = 3 finishes a whole residue class. This gives the order a tree search should
branch in: decide the small private moduli (3, 9, 27; 4, 18, 24, 48) first, since they are the only
ones that close whole subclasses, and treat everything coprime to 6 as shared filler.

**What the shared odd part can do.** Q alone leaves at least about 17% uncovered at lcm 3.4e7 by
greedy. If the true optimum is near that, classes 1 and 3 each need r-mass of at least 0.17 with no
overlap at all, and class 2 needs at least 1: rho_1 + rho_2 + rho_3 >= about 1.35 before any waste.
The whole of R sums to 1.55 at lcm 2e7. That margin (0.2 for the waste of three coverings) is the
reason nothing closes at this size, and it says what lcm to aim for: one where R sums to 2 or more.

**Can the reduction be relaxed?** Two relaxations give necessary conditions that are cheaper to test:
1. R alone must cover Z, or sigma_e > 0 (running: exact search, `results/ep273-r-only.txt`).
2. Q together with one copy of R must cover Z (true already at lcm 144).
Neither is sufficient. A sufficient relaxation is not available: the three classes genuinely share R.

Not proved here: any lower bound on waste beyond the inequality above, and any statement that a
covering does or does not exist.
