# Problem 273: what the structure looks like

Is there a covering system with distinct moduli m = p - 1, p >= 5 prime? Open; nothing here settles it.

## Reduction to three coupled coverings

Every modulus is even, and m = 4 covers one class mod 4, say x = 0. On a class x = 4w + c:

- m = 4r (so 4r + 1 is prime) lies inside one class mod 4 and acts on w as the modulus r;
- m = 2q with q odd (so 2q + 1 is prime) lies inside one parity and acts as the modulus q on both
  classes of that parity. The two residues differ by -1/2 mod q, which is one fixed translation of w
  for all such q together.

So a covering exists exactly when there are three coverings of the integers w:

1. class 2: moduli r from R = {r >= 3 : 4r + 1 prime} = {3, 4, 7, 9, 10, 13, 15, 18, 22, 24, ...},
   plus any odd q given to the even side;
2. classes 1 and 3: one shared set of odd q from Q = {q odd : 2q + 1 prime} = {3, 5, 9, 11, 15, 21, ...},
   and for each class its own set of r, the three sets of r pairwise disjoint.

A value can occur twice in one class (3 is in both R and Q), which is what makes closing a class easy.

## What exists at small lcm (`search/ep273/class_cover.py`, exact depth-first search)

- One odd class on its own is easy: nine moduli with lcm 144,
  r in {3, 4, 9, 18, 24, 48, 144} and q in {3, 9}. The search finds it in 9 nodes.
- A covering from R alone (the cheapest way to do class 2) was not found for lcm up to 90,720 within
  300,000 search nodes per lcm. That is "gave up", not "does not exist".

## Why the full problem is still hard

The three classes compete for R. The easy single-class covering spends 3, 4, 9, 18, 24, 48, 144, and
there is no second copy of those for the other two classes. The exhaustive result on the forum (no
covering with lcm below 8e6) says the three cannot be completed together at small lcm.

Budget in these terms: with S_R and S_Q the reciprocal sums of the two pools, a covering needs
S_R + 2 S_Q >= 3 plus overlap. At lcm 8.5e21 the pool gives about 4.68, so about 0.56 of overlap per
class is affordable. This is more room than the plain count (1.42 against 1) suggests, because every
odd q is used twice. It is the one encouraging number here.

## How far greedy placement gets (2026-11-18)

Greedy placement with local re-placement, one thread. These are lower bounds on the best coverage.

Each pool alone on one copy of Z/L (`greedy_cover.py`):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| odd q | 155,925 | 33 | 1.007 | 21.5% |
| odd q | 2,027,025 | 60 | 1.064 | 19.1% |
| odd q | 34,459,425 | 109 | 1.115 | 17.2% |
| r | 60,480 | 43 | 1.360 | 6.1% |
| r | 907,200 | 80 | 1.447 | 4.1% |
| r | 19,958,400 | 160 | 1.554 | 2.5% |

The coupled problem, all three copies (`joint_cover.py`):

| L (in w) | q | r | S_R + 2 S_Q | left uncovered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15,120 | 11 | 33 | 2.98 | 19.2% |
| 415,800 | 28 | 70 | 3.44 | 14.2% |
| 2,162,160 | 35 | 113 | 3.63 | 12.4% |
| 21,621,600 | 51 | 193 | 3.77 | 10.3% |

The last row is lcm 8.6e7 in the original moduli, and its 10.3% matches the 10 to 14 percent the forum
reports for local search at smaller lcm. Coverage improves by about two points per factor of ten in
the lcm. Nothing here is close to a covering, and nothing here shows one cannot exist.

## Not done

No exact search over the coupled problem, and no structured (tree) construction at large lcm, which
is what the numbers above say is needed. The even half in `half_cover.py` (cost 0.852) is
superseded by this split as the thing to optimise.
